Building Renewal Workflows That Actually Work in Epic

Ryan Headley March 7, 2026 5 min read

Renewals are the heartbeat of an insurance agency. They're also where most operational breakdowns happen. Missed follow-ups, policies that lapse because nobody caught the expiration date, renewal proposals that go out days before the deadline — these aren't just service failures. They're retention risks.

What a Broken Renewal Workflow Looks Like

The most common version I see: the agency relies on individual CSRs remembering what's due. There's a spreadsheet somewhere, updated inconsistently. Managers check in by asking questions instead of looking at data. When something falls through the cracks, the post-mortem is "we need to be more careful next time."

This isn't a people problem. It's a workflow problem.

What Epic Can Actually Do

Applied Epic has powerful built-in tools for renewal management — activities, suspenses, automated workflows — that most agencies haven't configured. When set up correctly, the system can:

  • Generate renewal activities automatically based on expiration dates
  • Route tasks to the right CSR or team without manual assignment
  • Track every step in the renewal process with timestamps and accountability
  • Escalate overdue items to management automatically
The goal is to remove memory from the equation. A well-designed renewal workflow means no one has to remember anything — the system does the remembering, and people do the work.

Designing the Right Workflow

There's no universal renewal workflow that works for every agency. The right design depends on your book of business, your team structure, and how you communicate with clients. That's exactly why generic templates fail — they're built for a hypothetical agency, not yours.

The starting point is always documentation: map out exactly how renewals should work today, identify every handoff point, and then design Epic to enforce that process — not a version of it, the actual process.
