Common Questions
What does the "—" stage mean and when does it appear?▼

The "—" stage is automatically assigned to any new or renewed policy when it is entered into Epic. It simply means no action has been taken yet on the renewal. It will remain on the Renewal Manager screen until someone initiates the renewal process or manually changes the stage.

How long do stages stay on the Renewal Manager screen?▼

All stages in Group 01 (stages --, 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 4a, 4b, 4c, 5a, 5b, 5c, and 6) remain on the Renewal Manager screen for 99 days past the policy's expiration date. Stages in Group 02 (7a, 7b, 7c, 7d, and 9) remove the policy from the screen immediately upon being set.

If a policy is canceled midterm, what stage should I use?▼

When you issue a cancellation in Epic, the system automatically sets the stage to 7d (Cancelled). From there, it is your responsibility to update the stage to the appropriate 7-series stage based on the reason: 7a for a cancel/rewrite, 7b if the insured no longer needs coverage, or 7c if the policy was brokered to another agency.

What is the difference between 5a, 5b, and 5c?▼

All three are "issued in Epic" stages but serve different purposes. 5a is used when a carrier download has been received and issued, but you want the CSR to do a check before moving to the final stage. 5b is for policies that auto-renewed but are still being marketed — you want to retain visibility that the incumbent is in hand. 5c is used after a bind request has been sent but the actual policy document has not yet been received from the carrier.

When should I use Stage 9 vs. Stage 5a?▼

Stage 9 is the true final stage — use it when the policy is fully issued, the document is received, and no further action is needed. It removes the policy from Renewal Manager entirely. Stage 5a is an intermediate step used when a commercial lines CSR wants to verify the download before closing out the renewal. Personal lines typically skips 5a and goes straight to 9.

Can I still see the policy after it's removed from Renewal Manager?▼

Yes. Removing a policy from the Renewal Manager screen does not delete any data. The stage is still recorded and visible when you navigate to the policy list on the account-level screen in Epic. Renewal Manager is simply a filtered working queue — removing from it just means the renewal is complete or resolved.
