Certificate Processing:
Daily vs. Renewal Batch

Account-level settings in CSR24 behave differently depending on whether you are issuing a single certificate or running a renewal batch. This guide covers what to confirm before each — and why getting it wrong can flood your insured's inbox.

Key Distinction

The Send a copy of all certificates to this insured field and the Renewal Subject / Message fields behave completely differently for daily versus renewal batch processing. Always set delivery to By Email and review these fields before processing — getting this wrong can send hundreds of duplicate emails to your insured in a single batch run.

Daily Certificates ↓ Renewal Batch ↓
Phase 01 Daily Certificates — Ad Hoc Issuance
1
ReviewSend copy to insured?
Confirm this field is set to By Email if the insured should receive a copy of the certificate, or No if not. Either setting is valid — the key is to verify it matches what the account requires before issuing.
Also check: Verify the confirmation email address is current before proceeding. A wrong address sends the certificate copy to the wrong party.
2
OnCoverage Validation Link
Keep this enabled. It sends a validation link with each emailed certificate that flags any coverage reductions that occur after the certificate is issued. This protects both the agency and the certificate holder from unknowingly relying on stale coverage information.
3
OnInsured Active? checkbox
This checkbox must be checked for the insured to have certificate processing permissions in the Self-Service Portal. If it is unchecked, the insured will not be able to access or initiate certificates through the portal.
4
ReviewCertificate Role Permissions
Confirm the assigned certificate role allows or restricts the correct actions for this account. Role permissions control whether the insured or CSR can:
  • Issue certificates
  • Add certificate holders
  • Edit the Description of Operations
  • Access additional endorsements or policy-level fields
Tip: Role permissions are set at the account level in CSR24. If a CSR reports they cannot perform an action during issuance, this is the first place to check.
5
ReviewConfirmation Email Address
Ensure the correct email address is entered in the confirmation field. A wrong address routes the certificate copy to the wrong party — often without any visible error at the time of issuance.
6
OnCertificate Instructions Pop-Up
If your organization has enabled certificate instructions, these display to the CSR at the time of issuance — before the certificate is sent. Use this feature to surface account-specific notes your team needs to see, such as custom Description of Operations language, known exclusions, or holder-specific requirements.
Tip: This feature must be enabled at the organization level before it becomes available at the account level. If you don't see it, check with your system administrator.
Phase 02 Renewal Batch — Bulk Issuance to All Active Holders
7
OffSend Copy to Insured?
Turn this OFF — set it to No — before running any renewal batch. This is the single most critical setting to check.
Why this matters: During a renewal batch, CSR24 issues certificates to every active holder on the account in a single run. If this field is still set to By Email, the insured receives one email per holder certificate — all at once, all with nearly identical subject lines. A large contractor or property manager account with 200+ holders will flood your insured's inbox with 200+ emails in minutes. This is the call you don't want to take.
8
ReviewRenewal Subject and Message
These fields control the email subject line and body that certificate holders receive during the renewal run. Review and customize them before processing begins.
If left blank: Holders receive generic, boilerplate messaging with no context about why they are receiving a certificate. Customized messaging looks professional, reduces holder confusion, and cuts down on inbound calls asking "why did I get this?"
9
ReviewRenewals / Batch Instructions
If your organization uses batch instructions, these display on the batch processing page before the run begins. Review them each renewal cycle to ensure they reflect any changes to the account — updated coverage lines, new holder requirements, or changes in Description of Operations language — since the previous year.
10
ReviewConfirmation Email Address
Verify the confirmation email address is current before running the batch. This check is especially important at renewal — staff changes happen over the course of a year, and if the address has not been updated, every confirmation from the batch run goes to the wrong person or to an inbox no one monitors.
Higher stakes at renewal: A wrong confirmation address for a single daily cert is a minor issue. The same wrong address during a renewal batch means every cert confirmation for the entire account is silently misrouted.
11
OnCoverage Validation Link
Keep this enabled for renewal batches. The validation link is sent with each certificate and flags coverage reductions after issuance.
Important limitation: The coverage validation link only checks policies that were manually added in CSR24. Policies added later through Applied Epic — after the batch is processed — will not be reflected in the validation results. Make sure all relevant policies are in place in CSR24 before running the batch.
12
ReviewCert Naming Structure
Confirm the PDF naming convention is correct before running the batch. Whatever naming structure is set at the account level will populate on every certificate the batch produces.
Fix it before, not after: Correcting a naming structure on a handful of daily certificates is manageable. Correcting it after a batch has issued 300 certificates is significantly more work — and in some cases requires manual cleanup for each holder. Spend the 30 seconds to confirm it now.
Phase 03 After the Batch — Restore Account Settings
13
Restore "Send Copy to Insured" to By Email
After the renewal batch is complete, restore the Send a copy of all certificates to this insured field back to By Email — if that is the correct setting for this account during normal daily processing.
Easy to forget: If this step is skipped, the next daily certificate issued for this account will not copy the insured, even though that was the expected behavior. The insured may not notice immediately — but when they do, it creates confusion about whether their certificates are being handled correctly.
Notes Important Notes for All Processing
A
Prevent Manual Policy Adds
Restrict Add Policies and Add New Policies on Templates permissions in System Admin. Manual policy additions in CSR24 bypass the normal Applied Epic workflow and can cause the coverage validation link to flag false results — or worse, miss genuine coverage changes because the policy wasn't added through Epic in the first place.
B
Confidential Clients
Only users who have confidential client access in Applied Epic can see those accounts in CSR24. Before running a renewal batch on a confidential account, verify that the CSR performing the run has the correct access level — otherwise the account may not appear or the run may fail partway through.
C
Use the Client View Tool Before a Renewal Run
The Client View button in the options bar shows the portal exactly as the insured sees it. Use this before a renewal batch to spot any access issues, missing policies, or configuration problems that would affect what the insured can see during and after the run — rather than discovering them after certificates have already been sent.
Tip: Client View is also useful for troubleshooting reports from insureds who say they cannot access their portal. Seeing what they see eliminates guesswork.
Common Questions
Why does the "Send copy to insured" setting cause so many problems at renewal? ▾

During daily processing, this setting sends one email per certificate — which is typically one or two at a time. The volume is low, so the behavior is easy to understand and manage. During a renewal batch, CSR24 issues certificates to every active holder on the account simultaneously. If the setting is still on, the system sends one email to the insured for every single holder cert it issues — all at the same time. A commercial account with 150 holders generates 150 emails in a matter of minutes. The setting is technically doing exactly what it's supposed to do, but the volume at batch scale makes it a serious operational problem.

What happens if I forget to restore "Send copy to insured" after the batch? ▾

The next daily certificate issued for that account will not copy the insured — even if they normally receive copies. The certificate will still be issued and sent to the holder, but the insured's copy will be skipped silently. Depending on the account, this may go unnoticed for days or weeks, or the insured may contact the agency wondering why they stopped receiving certificate copies. It's a small step that's easy to overlook in the post-batch wrap-up, which is why building it into a written checklist is worthwhile.

What is the coverage validation link actually checking? ▾

The coverage validation link checks whether the coverage described on the certificate at the time of issuance has changed since it was sent. If a policy is later cancelled, endorsements reduce limits, or coverage is otherwise modified, the link will flag that the certificate may no longer accurately reflect current coverage. This protects certificate holders from unknowingly relying on a certificate that no longer reflects the insured's actual coverage. The key limitation is that it only checks policies that were manually entered in CSR24 — not policies synced from Applied Epic after the certificate was issued.

Should I always customize the Renewal Subject and Message fields? ▾

Yes, for almost every account. The default messaging is generic and provides no context to the holder about why they are receiving a certificate. A customized subject line and message body — even a few sentences explaining that this is the annual renewal certificate for the policy period and who to contact with questions — significantly reduces holder confusion and inbound calls. It also presents the agency as organized and professional rather than running an automated process on autopilot. The fields are quick to fill in and the return on that effort is worth it.

Can I run a renewal batch for a confidential client if I don't have confidential access in Epic? ▾

No. Confidential client access in Applied Epic controls visibility in CSR24 as well. If you do not have confidential access, the account will not be accessible in CSR24, and you will not be able to run or manage a renewal batch for that account. Access must be granted in Applied Epic first — typically by a system administrator. Do not attempt to work around this restriction by having another user run the batch on your behalf without the appropriate access, as it can create audit and compliance issues.
